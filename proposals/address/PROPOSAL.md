# AI Hotel for Address Beach Resort
## A proposed service coordination pilot for Address Hotels + Resorts

**Prepared by AI Hotel · 5 October 2026 · Public discussion proposal**

Help guests receive clear answers, help teams carry requests through to completion, and help managers see the exceptions that need attention.

This independent proposal is intended for discussion with Address Beach Resort, JBR, Dubai, and Address Hotels + Resorts. It does not imply an affiliation, endorsement, agreed pilot or deployed integration. AI Hotel is at concept and validation stage; the capabilities below are proposed deliverables.

## Why this property

Address Beach Resort brings accommodation, beachfront leisure, dining, wellness and family experiences together in one destination. Its public room offering includes family, Club and suite options. These different experiences create a useful setting in which to evaluate context-aware service assistance.[1][2]

The proposal starts with the resort's approved service rules and its existing operating processes. A family planning a day, a guest requesting an amenity and a duty manager reviewing an outstanding case need different views of the same service journey.

Address already has digital guest initiatives: the brand has published a digital check-in experience at Address Downtown, while the Beach Resort's dining page links to established restaurant-booking channels.[3][4] Our discovery would establish how any new capability can complement the tools already used at JBR.

## What we propose to deliver

| Proposed workstream | Guest or team experience | Evidence of value |
|---|---|---|
| Approved-information concierge | Clear answers about services and entitlements, with a person available when a rule is uncertain | Accuracy against hotel-approved answers; successful handovers |
| Service-request coordination | One selected request category tracked from receipt to staff acknowledgement and completion | Completion time, repeat contacts and staff effort |
| Duty-manager brief | A concise view of outstanding cases, responsible teams and next actions | Brief preparation time, usefulness and visibility of overdue cases |

English and Russian are proposed evaluation languages. The property should determine the required language mix, including Arabic, before a live pilot.

### 1. Give guests answers they can rely on

Start with an approved set of hotel information: service availability, package benefits, family arrangements and routes to reservations or staff assistance. Each rule has an owner and review date.

**Illustrative guest request:** “We are staying with children. Which pool experience is suitable for our family?”

The proposed assistant would use the property's approved rules for the relevant date and guest category. If information is missing or conflicting, it would ask the responsible team. It would not invent eligibility or promise access.

### 2. Carry a request through to an outcome

Select one manageable service category with the hotel, such as amenity delivery.

**Illustrative request:** “Please bring two extra towels after 2 PM.”

The guest receives a clear acknowledgement. The responsible team accepts the request in the agreed workflow. A completion update is sent only when supported by the recorded outcome. Duplicate messages should not create duplicate work.

Requested timing remains a request until staff or an authorized system confirms it. If delivery is delayed, the guest and team see an accurate status and a route to assistance.

### 3. Give the manager a concise exception brief

A proposed brief would show which pilot cases remain open, which team owns the next action, and which cases need a decision. Each entry would link to its supporting record.

The brief would use only approved operational data. It would separate confirmed status from missing information and would not generate unsupported financial conclusions.

## A practical pilot sequence

| Phase | Work | Deliverable / decision |
|---|---|---|
| Discovery | Walk through a normal shift and an exception; review current tools, policies and request flow | Agreed problem, responsible sponsor, baseline and integration options |
| Demonstration | Use synthetic or approved redacted cases | Guest conversation, staff handover and manager brief for review |
| Staff evaluation | Run suggestions alongside the current process | Evidence of accuracy, usefulness and additional review effort |
| Supervised live pilot | One property, selected channel and request category, agreed hours and participation | Measured service outcomes with staff control |
| Review | Compare with baseline and existing alternatives | Continue, revise or stop; consider one additional property only if justified |

Duration and sample size would be agreed after reviewing request volume, access and staff availability. Discovery may show that an existing vendor feature or process improvement is the better solution.

## How success would be measured

Before a live pilot, agree the definitions and baseline for:

- accuracy of answers against approved policies;
- staff acknowledgement and request-completion time, including the slower cases;
- unresolved requests, repeat contacts and duplicate tasks;
- successful human handover and false confirmations;
- staff handling, review and correction effort;
- guest feedback, reported with participation and sample size.

The business case would include integration, support, messaging and supervision costs. Any released staff time would be distinguished from cash savings. No savings percentage, revenue uplift or purchase price is asserted in this proposal.

## Fit with current systems and service standards

The first demonstration can use synthetic cases and an approved information set. Any later connection to hotel systems depends on technical review, permissions and an agreed operating scope. The property's PMS, task platform and API availability have not been established by public research.

Staff retain control of sensitive decisions and guest-facing commitments. Payment, refund, pricing, access, safety and significant reservation decisions remain with authorized teams. The pilot should use minimum necessary data, agreed retention, appropriate access controls and a clear stop procedure.

A paid booking is confirmed only through the authorized reservation process. Submitting a request is not the same as securing a table, appointment, room or upgrade.

## From JBR to the wider Address portfolio

The Address portfolio spans several markets and property types.[5] A successful JBR evaluation could support a reusable approach to approved information, service ownership and outcome measurement.

Expansion would require property-specific policies, language coverage, access rights and commercial approval. Shared branding does not establish a common system stack or permission to share guest data. Palace properties, branded residences and planned openings should be scoped separately.

## Proposed next conversation

A 60-minute working session with property operations and the relevant technology representative:

1. Follow one frequent guest request from arrival to completion.
2. Review one manager report and one unresolved service case.
3. Compare the proposed workflow with existing software capabilities.
4. Select a small evaluation scope and agree who approves its information.
5. Define the evidence needed for a costed pilot proposal.

**The proposed outcome: an agreed, measurable way to test whether AI Hotel can give Address teams more time for attentive service.**
